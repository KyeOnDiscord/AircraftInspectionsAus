Private Versus Air Transport Category Certification

When considering aircraft purchase, the purpose for which it will be utilised needs to be considered carefully, as the aircraft certification at time of purchase will determine its capabilities and limitations. Amendment of aircraft certification post-acquisition may prove cost prohibitive and impractical.

Piston powered aircraft are most likely to be certified in either Private or Air Transport categories.

Private category certification provides far more flexibility and economy regarding stipulated maintenance procedures, however, the aircraft can only be used for private travel. The aircraft cannot be used for commercial purposes or as per wording stipulated in regulatory text: “Not for Hire or Reward”

To explore this further, there is nothing to prohibit Cost Share of an aircraft which is certified in Private category. Cost share is where the costs of operating the aircraft is shared by the owner, crew and passengers. For example, if operating costs to fly between departure and destination will be to the amount of say one thousand dollars in order to pay for fuel, scheduled maintenance, airport and airways charges, a pilot and three passengers can share these charges and contribute $250 each to this flight expense. However, no party can profiteer from these payment contributions.

An aircraft which is certified in the Private category is also subject to less stringent maintenance standards, such as permissible engine utilisation extensions once the engine has reached operational limitations.

By contrast, an aircraft certified in the Air Transport category is subject to a more stringent and subsequently more costly system of maintenance due to the fact that it can be used to carry fare paying passengers.

You need to decide if your aircraft will be used for Private or Air Transport operations before you commence shopping.
